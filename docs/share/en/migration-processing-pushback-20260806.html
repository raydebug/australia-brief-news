Labor's migration overhaul meets pushback from business and MPs

The federal government is weighing changes to skilled and family visa processing as it tries to slow net overseas migration, with a permanent intake target of 185,000 still in place. Reports say Tony Burke has delayed a major speech while ministers consider giving priority to people already in Australia. The debate matters because business groups fear tighter settings will worsen labour shortages while Labor faces pressure from the Coalition, One Nation and some of its own MPs. The next step is how far the government is willing to tighten the system without triggering a broader backlash.
