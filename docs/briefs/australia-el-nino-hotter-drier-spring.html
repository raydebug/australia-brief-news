El Niño surge puts much of Australia on track for a hotter, drier spring

The latest Bureau outlook points to below-average rain across much of eastern and southern Australia and above-average heat from August into spring. Meteorologists say the Pacific has warmed fast enough to raise the risk of record warmth, stronger fire conditions and more pressure on farms and water supplies. The story matters because the run into spring is when dry landscapes can shift quickly into bushfire weather. The forecast still has uncertainty, especially around how much rain falls in the tropics and western regions.
