Parliament starts a wartime preparedness inquiry for the first time since World War II

A new bipartisan committee will examine how Australia could keep essential goods, services and government functions going if a conflict hit the region. Defence Minister Richard Marles says the country has neglected resilience planning for decades, and the committee will look at gaps in civil preparedness, continuity of government and support for national defence. It matters because ministers are openly treating regional security as a practical planning problem, not just a military one. The next step is public submissions before the committee reports later in the year.
