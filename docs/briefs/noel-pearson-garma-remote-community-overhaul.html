Noel Pearson urges federal control of remote Aboriginal communities

Noel Pearson used his Garma Festival keynote to argue that remote Aboriginal communities should move under Commonwealth oversight and that state and territory Aboriginal affairs departments should be dismantled. He framed the change as a way to fix policy drift and budget waste after the Voice referendum defeat. It matters because Garma is one of the main national stages for Indigenous policy, and the speech pushes the debate toward governance and accountability rather than symbolism. The next question is whether governments treat the proposal as a serious reform path or just another festival headline.
