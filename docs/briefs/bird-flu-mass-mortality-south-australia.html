South Australia's bird flu outbreak appears to be entering a mass-mortality phase

More than 80 dead and sick greater crested terns have been found off South Australia's coast near Robe, and authorities say the pattern points to the first suspected mass-mortality event linked to H5 bird flu in Australia. The same outbreak has now also reached Victoria, where a greater crested tern in Portland tested positive, and South Australian surveillance has also raised concerns about spread inland. It matters because the virus is no longer confined to a handful of seabird detections; it is starting to move through wildlife populations that can spread it further. The next step is continued surveillance, with officials still saying the risk to poultry and people remains low for now.
