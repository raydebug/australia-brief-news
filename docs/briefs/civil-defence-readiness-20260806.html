Australia opens a civil-defence readiness review after decades of neglect

A new federal inquiry will examine how Australia would keep fuel, supplies, government services and business functions moving if a conflict disrupted the country. The committee has been asked to look beyond the military itself and consider how civilian agencies and the private sector would work together in a crisis. That matters because Australia has not done a broad national readiness review like this since the Second World War. The next step is submissions and evidence that show where the biggest weak points are, especially around supply chains and coordination.
