Renewable projects are losing trust in regional NSW

A NSW inquiry and a national survey both point to the same problem for renewable projects: regional trust is slipping. One report says community engagement in the state's renewable zones fell short, while the other shows anti-wind and anti-solar campaigns have weakened support more broadly. That matters because social licence can slow projects as much as engineering does, and governments now have to prove local communities benefit from the buildout.

Source differences

  • ABC focuses on the NSW inquiry's finding that engagement with impacted communities fell short, while Guardian emphasises the survey evidence that anti-wind and anti-solar campaigns have changed public attitudes.
  • Both point to a trust problem around renewable rollout, but they highlight different evidence streams.
