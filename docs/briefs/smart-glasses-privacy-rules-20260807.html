Australia moves to tighten smart-glasses privacy oversight

The federal attorney-general has asked the privacy commissioner to look at smart glasses after growing concern about discreet recording and surveillance. Regulators say the devices can capture people without them realising it, and the warning is strongest around women, children and other vulnerable groups. The immediate question is whether Australia can update the law before wearable cameras and face-analysis tools become more common. The next step is a test of whether existing privacy rules are enough, or whether they now need a faster rewrite.
