High Court restores the tougher Emma Lovell sentence

The High Court has restored the harsher punishment for the man who killed Brisbane mother Emma Lovell during a Boxing Day home invasion. The case turns on whether the reduced non-parole period was justified for a teenager who was 17 at the time of the offence and later pleaded guilty to murder and related charges. It matters because the ruling feeds Queensland's wider youth-crime debate and shows how far courts are willing to go when deciding whether special circumstances should change a serious homicide sentence. The next step is the family's continued wait for closure while the sentencing debate continues in politics and the courts.
