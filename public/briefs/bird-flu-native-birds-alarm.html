Wild bird deaths raise new alarm over Australia's bird flu outbreak

Authorities are investigating more than 80 dead and sick greater crested terns on South Australia's Baudin Rocks, along with suspected inland cases, as H5N1 spreads through native birds. The latest warnings matter because the outbreak is no longer confined to a narrow coastal cluster and may be moving deeper into wildlife populations. Experts say the human risk remains low, but the ecological risk is rising fast if the virus keeps spreading. The next step is laboratory confirmation and close monitoring of other bird colonies and species.
