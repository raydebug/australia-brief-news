Scientists uncover a rare thylacine femur near Alice Springs

Researchers at the Alcoota fossil beds north-east of Alice Springs have identified an intact femur from the thylacine, the old bone-crunching marsupial predator. The bone is about 8 million years old and is the first complete femur of its kind found at the site. It matters because a better limb fossil can help scientists understand how the species moved and how it fit into Australia's ancient ecosystems. The remaining question is what other clues the fossil beds still hold.
