Australia's bird-flu response widens from Christmas Island to wildlife risk warnings

The federal government is spending more than $700,000 to prepare Christmas Island for a possible H5 bird flu outbreak, while new risk assessments say hundreds of native bird and mammal species are highly exposed if the virus spreads. That matters because Australia's response is moving from broad warnings to concrete planning for remote island facilities and vulnerable wildlife. The key question is whether biosecurity and habitat protections can be scaled fast enough before the virus reaches more places.
