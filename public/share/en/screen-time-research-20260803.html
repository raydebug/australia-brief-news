New research cools the alarm over children's screen time

New research suggests parents should not treat everyday screen time as a direct cause of developmental delay. That matters because it pushes back on a common parenting fear and suggests the bigger issues are content, context and family routines rather than the screen count alone. The next question is how health advice will balance caution with evidence that the simple time total only tells part of the story.
