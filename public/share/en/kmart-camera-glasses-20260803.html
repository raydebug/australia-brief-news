Kmart's budget camera glasses draw privacy warnings

Kmart has sold out of its $89 Anko camera glasses, putting a cheap pair of wearable cameras into more Australian hands. The glasses can record video and still images, and privacy advocates say that makes it easier to film people without consent in shops, transport and other public places. That matters because the device lowers the cost of covert recording while privacy rules are still catching up. The next question is whether retailers, regulators and shoppers treat it as a normal gadget or a new surveillance problem.
